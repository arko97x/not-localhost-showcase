The Story Behind The Project

Catch a Predator (CaP)

Meet Cappy, the pocket companion of CaP (short for Catch a Predator). Built with an ESP32-S3, a 1.28″ round LCD, an accelerometer knock detector, and an ERM haptic motor inside a 3D-printed vibrant orange squircle case, Cappy sniffs out nearby Meta Ray-Bans and AR smart glasses in real time.

01 Origin • Orientation Week

A Prelude to the Problem

Right before the start of the studio during orientation week, Vineeta made us participate in an exercise where we had to anticipate where we see ourselves 5 years from now and write a newspaper article in this hypothetical future talking about ourselves. Reading about recent developments in tech, I was disillusioned and pessimistic about what a designer would be working on 5 years down the line that would end up in the news cycle.

Daily News
21st July 2033 • FUTURE HIT PIECE
Young Entrepreneur Unveils Questionable Product: AI and AR Smart Glasses For Cats
Moulik Nanda, the founder of XYZ LLC, has decided to step into the pet care industry now with a questionable product launch... I feel like the penchant for being overly complex or shifting focus has led to a deprecating problem. Not only does this product hamper the cat's natural hunting instincts, it also impacts their cognition. Another issue that arises with this is privacy. As if putting cameras on everyone's faces wasn't bad enough, putting them on faces of little cats who can unsuspectingly get into anywhere is a major safety issue, especially for women.
Historical Parallels

The Luddite Movement & The Hammer

Starting this studio by researching the Luddite movement and its foundations made me think about recent developments in tech and my speculative article once again.

Further research helped me draw parallels: from smart glasses being used by perverts to non-consensually record women, to glasses sending all recordings back to Meta (who employ people in impoverished countries just to look at the recordings and train AI). There were way too many demerits for this product to exist in the first place, let alone be the smartphone replacement that Mark Zuckerberg desperately wants it to be.

“While public perception has gotten negative, we still did not have a hammer for this server room. My next goal was to come up with it.”
02 Framework • Galloway & Ratto

Critical Making, Protocol & The 3 Hammers

We were introduced to Matt Ratto’s paper on Critical Making and Alexander R. Galloway’s Protocol. Tactical media introduced me to a brand new form of resistance: exploiting flaws in the protocol itself to your advantage. If I open-source this project and share how I made it, it becomes tactical media against big corporations like Meta.

Idea 03 • Chosen Hammer!

CAP: Catch a Pervert

A physical wearable companion that continuously analyses nearby Bluetooth LE signals, extracts 16-bit SIG manufacturer IDs, and alerts the wearer with expressive eyes and haptics when glasses lurk nearby.

✓ Puts control of privacy back into your hands.
03 Prototyping • From SP Road to Fusion 360

The Making of Cappy

I wanted the product to be as fashionable as it was functional. I took inspiration from a device on my moodboard—aptly named lilguy.net—and decided to create a companion creature of my own living inside my product: Cappy.

01

The SP Road Shopping Spree

Bangalore Electronics Market

I started by doing a shopping spree at SP Road, where I got my first ESP32. After cycling an onboard RGB LED, I tried to write code to detect my Apple Watch by explaining the product to an LLM. My first attempts failed due to my lack of knowledge of modern Bluetooth advertising protocols.

02

Open-Source Breakthrough: Nearby Glasses

GitHub Discovery

Modern devices don’t continuously advertise themselves in simple ways (especially from companies like Meta). I found Nearby Glasses, an open-source Android app on GitHub that sniffs BLE advertisements for registered company identifiers. I adapted its logic to my ESP32, mapping RSSI proximity to LED colors and testing it in the real world with my own earphones (Nothing Earbuds).

03

Cappy V1 & The Fusion 360 Jumper Wire Jam

First 3D Print

I wanted a squircle with a circular hole in the middle for the 1.28″ round LCD and a hole on the top right for a button. I spent hours learning Fusion 360 on YouTube and measuring parts with measuring tape. Unfortunately, I forgot to factor in the space that jumper wires take up! The case couldn't shut, and with no cut-out for the USB port, I had to keep Cappy V1 propped open to use him.

04

Cappy 2.0: Bigger, Better, Haptics & Knock Detection

Final Srishti Prototype

After feedback from Manuel to make Cappy more interactive and slightly bigger for better functionality, Cappy 2.0 was born.

  • ADXL345 Accelerometer: Integrated as a “knock detector”—knock on the enclosure while Cappy is asleep to wake him up groggy.
  • Vibration Motor + MOSFET: Tactile feedback—a sharp buzz on curious, double buzz on angry, and aggressive triple pulse on rage mode.
  • Redesigned Orange Squircle Case: Secure snap connection, display support brackets, dedicated USB-C charging slot, and power switch.
  • The Soldering Sagas: “Without support from Nataraju and Sudarsini, I would have fried my ESP32. Do not use superglue as an alternative for solder in emergency situations (long story).”
04 The Climax • A3 Presentation Day

The Meta Glasses Heist & Live Demo

With Cappy 2.0 ready, there was only one thing left: procuring real Meta glasses for the final A3 demonstration. This became an adventure of its own.

The Heist Saga

  • Lead 2 (JP Nagar): Disappeared the exact second I mentioned travelling to Yelahanka!
  • Lead 3: Utilised even shadier methods to procure never-before-seen, unreleased versions of the Meta Ray-Bans for the demonstration!
The Final Demo

Sudarsini: The “Seasoned Pervert”

I structured my presentation so Cappy could speak for himself. A minimal booth with my poster and Cappy on my body.

I demoed CAP in front of the class by calling in my peer Sudarsini playing the “seasoned pervert” wearing the Meta glasses. As she walked closer, Cappy’s expressions shifted from curious yellow to angry red, and the vibration motor buzzed in real time! Once the pervert was gone, Cappy dozed off, and I demoed the knock detection to wake him back up.

06 Under The Hood • Firmware & CLI

Technical Specifications & Live CLI

Cappy is powered by an ESP32-S3-Zero running dual-core FreeRTOS firmware: Core 0 is dedicated to 100% duty-cycle NimBLE scanning, while Core 1 renders 30 FPS spring-physics graphics via LovyanGFX over 40MHz SPI.

07 Post-Presentation Observations

Reflections & The Future

Working on CAP and Cappy has been one of the most intensive and transdisciplinary projects I have worked on at Srishti. While I was already aware of how art and politics go hand in hand, the practice of critical making takes the connections even further with the conversations it sparks.

Feedback & Observations

Areas to Iterate Upon

  • Locking & Weight: Given the size and weight of Cappy, he tended to sag when worn on a shirt, meaning I had to frequently hold him up so the screen faced forward. Future versions need a more robust clip or magnetic mount.
  • Range Calibration: Depending on the room and background 2.4 GHz noise, detection range varied from <1m to 3.5m. Further RSSI threshold filtering is planned.
  • Wearability: Cappy works equally well clipped to a waistband for discrete alerts or worn proudly on a shirt for public awareness.
Next Steps

Towards Capstone & Open Source

I do not feel like I’m done with this project. I have so many ideas waiting to be executed for my capstone next semester: miniaturised custom PCBs, injection-molded modular shells, and full open-source tactical media kits so anyone can assemble their own Cappy and fight back.
